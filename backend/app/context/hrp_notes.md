# HRP-style mission health risks (summary)

**Verify this summary against official NASA Human Research Program (HRP) pages before clinical or operational use. This file is a short orientation brief, not a protocol and not a source of clinical thresholds.**

## Radiation
Deep-space and vehicle environments expose crews to ionizing radiation beyond typical Earth levels. Mission planning emphasizes shielding, monitoring dose, and watching for early physiological stress responses. Exact exposure limits and medical criteria belong in NASA HRP / flight-surgeon guidance, not here.

## Isolation and confinement
Long missions separate crews from familiar support networks. Mood, sleep quality, and interpersonal strain can drift. Daily check-ins and plain-language check-ins with the medical team help surface changes early.

## Circadian disruption
Artificial light–dark cycles, night work, and short sleep windows can disrupt circadian rhythm. Fatigue and slower recovery often show up before a clear “diagnosis.” Rest planning and sleep hygiene matter more than guessing a disease name.

## Microgravity / fluid shifts
Weightlessness redistributes body fluids and can change how cardiovascular readings look versus Earth baselines. Interpret blood pressure and related vitals against the astronaut’s personal baseline, and escalate dizziness or fainting concerns to the medical team.

## How VITALIS should use this
Treat the above as mission context only. Always say “possible,” never diagnose or prescribe, and if unsure, refer to the medical / flight-surgeon team.
